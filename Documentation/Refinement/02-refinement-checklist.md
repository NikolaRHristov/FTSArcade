## (c) Mock-review prompt set

**Comprehension test first** (unfamiliar reader answers without looking back at the draft; mismatches = drafting defects):

1. Who in San Francisco benefits?
2. What problem does the project address?
3. What does the team actually deliver?
4. Why should that produce the claimed outcomes?
5. Why is the requested amount justified?
6. What remains after funding ends?

**Then score all four criteria (1-5), each with a comment, using the official anchors in 00-alignment-matrix.md.** Four independent readers; use disagreements to find unclear claims, not to predict the official result - a small sample must not be normalized (export).

**Reviewer prompts (export's simulation set) with the passing response each must elicit:**

| Prompt | Passing response |
|---|---|
| Who changes, by how much, and how will that be known? | Names the population, the result, and the measure (indicator + instrument + timing). |
| What must be true for the mechanism to work? | Identifies assumptions explicitly, without inventing new ones. |
| What can be delivered in the first year? | Separates first-year capacity from eventual scale; nothing exceeds named capacity. |
| Where is San Francisco execution dependent on others? | Names dependencies and their status (confirmed / in discussion / proposed). |
| Which claim is least supported? | Matches the evidence register - the reviewer's answer equals the team's known weakest grade. |
| What cost could be removed without changing outcomes? | Identifies a real efficiency, or confirms the budget is already minimal - proportionality demonstrated either way. |
| What survives if funding stops? | Names a retained asset, its owner, and its operating need. |

**Also ask each reader:** the strongest substantiated claim; the most important unsupported assumption; the main execution risk; and **what evidence would justify the next-higher score per criterion** - that last answer is the alignment feedback loop, because reviewer comments in the real process justify every score (evaluationprocess, verified).

**Commercial-benefit test (export) for every business feature before it can appear even as a sustainability mechanism:** What charitable result does it produce? Who pays and who benefits? Would it still belong if it generated no revenue? Could it advantage the company/advertisers more than the public? What rule prevents excessive private benefit? What happens to public access if advertising disappears?

**Language blacklist (export):** "revolutionary / world-class / unique / cutting-edge"; "empower / unlock / transform" without a defined change; "scalable" without a unit cost and constraint; "community-led" without a defined community authority; "sustainable" without a payer or operating model; "partners include" when discussions are incomplete; "data shows" when the source is an assumption; "at scale" without a number; "and more"; "solutions for San Francisco" without naming the population and condition.

**Uncertainty vocabulary to enforce in the draft:** "As of [date], [source] shows…" (fact) · "In [period], the project produced…" (team result) · "Comparable evidence suggests…" (analogy) · "The project will test whether…" (assumption) · "The team is in discussion with…" (partnership) · "The lead will…" (future action) · "The planning target is…" (target). Editing may shorten but never upgrade certainty.
